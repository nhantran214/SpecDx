\subsection{Auditing benchmarks and executable specifications}

A growing body of work shows that the checks of widely used benchmarks are often wrong.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Code benchmarks.} Test-based evaluation misses many wrong programs~\cite{liu2023your}. In SWE-bench, many patches that pass the tests are incorrect or exploit weak tests~\cite{wang2026solved,yu2025utboost,yu2026swe}.
\item \textbf{Agent benchmarks.} Annotation errors and underspecified tasks have been reported in $\tau$-bench~\cite{cuadron2025saber}, in agentic benchmarks in general~\cite{zhu2025establishing}, in tool-calling evaluations~\cite{bhat2026benchmarking} and in data-engineering benchmarks~\cite{zanoli2026elt}. Cao et al.\ show that policy ambiguity often masquerades as agent error~\cite{cao2026policy}.
\item \textbf{Label errors.} Even established test sets contain them, and they can change model rankings~\cite{northcutt2021pervasive,gema2025done}.
\end{itemize}
Most of these audits are manual. Recent tools automate them with a strong LLM as reviewer. BenchGuard reviews instructions, tests and solutions with an LLM protocol~\cite{tu2026who}. Other auditors check tasks for hidden dependencies and grading gaps~\cite{wang2026automated}, inspect benchmark components~\cite{suh2026agentsuite} or analyse agent transcripts for flaws~\cite{mohl2026automated}.

A related line of work, failure attribution, asks which agent and step caused a failed run~\cite{zhang2025which,cemri2025multi,ge2026spectrum}. These methods take the checks as given and blame an agent.

\specdx shares the goal of the auditors but differs in method. It does not ask one model whether a check is right. It infers what $\Tell$ admits from how a calibrated population \emph{executes} it, and it backs each finding with a logical witness. It also asks the question that failure attribution leaves open: when should the blame go to the specification? We compare against BenchGuard (B1) and an adapted Who\&When (B2) directly.

\subsection{Ambiguity and inconsistency in requirements}

Requirements engineering has long studied ambiguity and inconsistency in natural-language requirements~\cite{berry2003contract,chantree2006identifying,gervasi2005reasoning,femmer2017rapid}. LLMs are now used to detect inconsistent requirements~\cite{fantechi2023inconsistency}, to find contradictions through formal logic~\cite{gartner2024automated} and to audit requirements neurosymbolically~\cite{hall2026neurosymbolic}. In business processes, ambiguity has been detected by executing a generated process model~\cite{matei2026ambiguity}. These methods analyse the text, or one formal model of it. They do not observe how several executors read it, and they do not use the checks as evidence.

The closest work combines execution with disagreement:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item ClarifyGPT and SpecFix sample programs from one LLM and call a requirement ambiguous when the programs disagree~\cite{mu2024clarifygpt,jia2025automated}.
\item JEST runs several JavaScript engines and blames the specification when most of them fail the same test~\cite{park2021jest}.
\end{itemize}
The first treats every disagreement as ambiguity and has no notion of the model's competence. The second assumes near-deterministic executors and does not type its findings. \specdx separates disagreement caused by interpreter error from disagreement caused by the text, and reports four defect types. We compare against the JEST rule directly.

\subsection{Learning from fallible raters}

\paragraph{Truth inference.} Inferring a true label from fallible raters goes back to Dawid and Skene~\cite{dawid1979maximum}. GLAD added item difficulty~\cite{whitehill2009whose}, and later work refined the estimators~\cite{hovy2013learning,zheng2017truth}. Identifiability of such latent class models rests on conditionally independent views~\cite{allman2009identifiability}. Some models allow several true values, or none: multi-truth discovery~\cite{zhao2012bayesian,wang2015integrated}, models of whether a truth exists~\cite{zhi2015modeling}, and top-two recovery in crowdsourcing~\cite{jeong2023recovering}. Errors of different LLMs are correlated~\cite{kim2025correlated,goel2025great}, much as coincident failures were in multi-version software~\cite{knight1986experimental}.

\paragraph{Item response theory and label errors.} IRT weighs benchmark items by difficulty and discrimination~\cite{lalor2016building,rodriguez2021evaluation}. Land and Bikel use it to audit LLM benchmarks: an item whose responses fit a model with a forced ceiling is flagged as likely mislabelled~\cite{land2026auditing}. Confident learning and LLM-based detectors flag label errors from model predictions~\cite{northcutt2021confident,nahum2025llms,klie2023annotation}.

\paragraph{Disagreement as a signal.} Semantic entropy measures uncertainty over the meanings of sampled answers~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024detecting}. Disagreement between models has been used to stress-test model specifications~\cite{zhang2025stress}, and an agent's disagreement with itself serves as an uncertainty signal~\cite{mehta2026agents}.

\paragraph{What \specdx adds.} These methods either assume one correct answer per item, or measure disagreement without explaining it. The anchored cell model combines set-valued truth with ability and difficulty, and adds two things:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item an \emph{anchor}: the specification is one more rater, whose declared value enters the prior;
\item an error distribution truncated to values \emph{outside} the truth.
\end{itemize}
Together they give the accuracy-gradient signatures of \cref{lem:signatures}, which separate ambiguity and silence from concentrated errors. We compare against GLAD and Dawid--Skene (TI), the IRT audit (B3) and semantic entropy (B4) directly.

\subsection{Logical diagnosis and test design}

\paragraph{Conflicts and diagnosis.} Reiter's theory of diagnosis~\cite{reiter1987theory} and de Kleer and Williams' work on multiple faults~\cite{kleer1987diagnosing} established the duality between conflicts and diagnoses. Liffiton and Sakallah turned it into practical MUS enumeration~\cite{liffiton2008algorithms,liffiton2016fast}. Unsatisfiable cores also debug overconstrained declarative specifications~\cite{shlyakhter2003debugging,torlak2008finding}. Diagnosis has been extended to multiple~\cite{ignatiev2019model} and uncertain~\cite{cazes2020model} observations, but the observations still come from the system, not from fallible readers. \specdx treats each LLM run as a \emph{noisy} sample of a correction set, weights it by the run's estimated faithfulness, and confirms candidates by sound refutation. It localises ambiguity with delta debugging~\cite{zeller2002simplifying} over the sentences of $\Tell$, with a statistical oracle.

\paragraph{Oracle quality and specification mutation.} The oracle problem~\cite{barr2015oracle} asks whether checks are correct and strong enough. Oracle assessment finds checks that are too weak~\cite{jahangirova2016test,schuler2011assessing}, and vacuity detection finds properties that hold for the wrong reason~\cite{kupferman2003vacuity}. Specification mutation~\cite{black2000mutation,jia2011analysis} has recently been used to assess formal specifications~\cite{amaral2026mutdafny}. Specification errors are known to cause real bugs even in verified systems~\cite{fonseca2017empirical}. Our evaluation adopts specification mutation with kill witnesses; \specdx itself targets specifications whose Tell part is natural language.

\paragraph{Choosing tests.} Domain testing places inputs at the boundaries of conditions~\cite{white1980domain}. Choosing the next experiment is a problem of Bayesian experimental design~\cite{rainforth2024modern}, and Thompson sampling~\cite{thompson1933likelihood,russo2018tutorial} and active testing~\cite{kossen2021active} offer practical policies. \specdx combines boundary scenarios taken from the literals of $\Tell$ with Thompson sampling over scenario cells, rewarded by the change in its own defect posteriors.
